[Plotinus](../lineage/plotinus.md)'s argument is a regress about unity. Anything
that exists is *some one thing*; but a composite owes its unity to something
else; so the source of all unity cannot itself be composite, and cannot be a
being among beings. The One (*to hen*) is therefore "beyond being"
(*epekeina tês ousias*, taking *Republic* 509b at its word).

## The break with Aristotle

This is the sharpest disagreement in the ancient tradition about what the first
principle is like, and it is worth stating precisely. Aristotle's
[unmoved mover](../aristotle/unmoved-mover.md) is *noêsis noêseôs* — thought
thinking itself, and hence maximally actual. Plotinus objects: thinking, even
self-thinking, involves a distinction between thinker and thought, and so is
*already duality*. A first principle must be prior to that. So Aristotle's god
is demoted to second place — Plotinus's *Nous*, the level of Intellect where
being and thought coincide — and something simpler is placed above it.

The consequence is a via negativa. The One is not a being, not a substance, not
good (it is the Good, which is not the same), not knowable, and strictly not
nameable; even calling it "one" is a concession.

## Downstream

- **[Emanation](neoplatonic-emanation.md)** — how multiplicity comes from
  simplicity without the One's diminishing or choosing.
- **[Divine simplicity](divine-simplicity.md)** — the Christian and Islamic
  reworkings, where the first principle is identified with being itself rather
  than placed beyond it, which is a substantive change.
- Every subsequent claim that [ultimate
  reality](ultimate-explanation.md) is ineffable, and every argument that such
  a claim is self-undermining.
