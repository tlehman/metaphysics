Three distinct arguments share the name, and their premises differ enough that
objections to one often miss the others.

## Kalām (from a temporal beginning)

1. Whatever begins to exist has a cause.
2. The universe began to exist.
3. So the universe has a cause.

Al-Ghazālī's, revived by Craig. Premise 2 is defended by arguments against an
actual infinite and, contingently, by big-bang cosmology. Notably *not*
Aristotle's, who held the world eternal — his mover is required despite the
absence of a beginning, not because of one.

## Thomistic (from causal or existential dependence)

The series in question is not temporal but *hierarchical*: a chain of
simultaneous dependence, where each member's causing depends on the one above it
now. Such a series cannot regress infinitely without there being no causing at
all, so it terminates in something whose causing is underived. Aquinas's *prima
via* is [Aristotle's argument](../aristotle/unmoved-mover.md) — nothing moves
itself, an infinite regress of *per se* movers explains nothing — with the
conclusion converted from a cause of motion to a cause of
[being](essence-existence-distinction.md), via the claim that whatever has
essence distinct from existence must be caused to exist.

## Leibnizian (from contingency)

1. Everything that exists has an explanation, either in the necessity of its own
   nature or in an external cause ([PSR](principle-of-sufficient-reason.md)).
2. The totality of contingent things exists.
3. Its explanation cannot be contingent, so it lies in something necessary.

## Standing objections

- **Deny the PSR** and premise 1 collapses; brute facts are cheap.
- **The taxicab objection** (Schopenhauer): why does the causal principle stop
  where the argument wants it to? The Thomist's answer is that it stops at what
  is [pure actuality](pure-actuality.md), where the question "what causes it?"
  has no content — not that it is dismissed.
- **Composition.** Hume and Russell: explaining every member may exhaust the
  explanatory demand, leaving nothing owed to the collection.
- **Van Inwagen's necessitarian dilemma** — see the
  [PSR](principle-of-sufficient-reason.md).
- The conclusion, even granted, delivers a necessary being, not obviously a
  personal one — [Aristotle's god](../aristotle/unmoved-mover.md) is the
  cautionary case: eternal, necessary, and entirely uninterested in the world.
