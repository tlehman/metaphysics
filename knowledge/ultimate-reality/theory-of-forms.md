[Plato](../lineage/plato.md)'s Forms are posited to do several jobs at once, and
much of the criticism turns on whether one thing can do all of them:

- **Semantic** — what a general term refers to.
- **Epistemic** — the objects of *epistêmê* as opposed to opinion, since
  knowledge requires a stable object and sensibles do not stay put.
- **Explanatory** — a thing is beautiful *because* it participates in Beauty.
- **Normative** — the Form of the Good is "beyond being" and is what makes the
  other Forms knowable (*Republic* VI, 509b).

The Forms are separate (*chôris*), which is the crucial word: they exist whether
or not anything instantiates them. That is what makes them
[transcendent universals](../being/realism-about-universals.md), and what makes
Plato the ancestor of *ante rem*
[structuralism](../mathematics/structuralism.md) and of
[mathematical platonism](../mathematics/mathematical-platonism.md).

## Aristotle's objections

The *Metaphysics* argues against them throughout, in Α.9 and Μ–Ν most
systematically:

- **Explanatory idleness.** Forms are "eternal sensibles"; positing a second
  world duplicates the problem instead of solving it, and contributes nothing to
  the knowledge or the coming-to-be of things.
- **The Third Man.** If Man and particular men are all *F* by participation in a
  Form, a further Form is needed to explain what they share — regress.
- **Λ.6's objection**, which is the deepest: eternal substances are useless
  unless something in them can cause change, and even an active substance is
  insufficient if its substance is a *capacity*. The Forms are inert. See
  [the unmoved mover](../aristotle/unmoved-mover.md).
- **Separation is unearned.** What is genuinely separate is
  [substance](../aristotle/substance.md); mathematicals and universals are
  considered *as* separated, which is a different thing
  ([abstractionism](../aristotle/mathematical-abstractionism.md)).

Plato's own *Parmenides* raises the participation and regress problems first,
which is why "Plato's theory of Forms" is better read as a research programme
than a fixed doctrine.
