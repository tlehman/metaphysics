[Leibniz](../lineage/leibniz.md) puts it in *The Principles of Nature and
Grace* (1714): "why is there something rather than nothing? For nothing is
simpler and easier than something." The question is the
[PSR](principle-of-sufficient-reason.md) applied to the totality, and it is the
limiting case of [ultimate explanation](ultimate-explanation.md).

## The main lines of reply

- **There is a necessary being.** Leibniz's own answer, and the
  [cosmological argument](cosmological-argument.md)'s: the reason for the
  contingent series lies outside it, in something whose essence involves
  existence ([essence/existence](essence-existence-distinction.md),
  [pure actuality](pure-actuality.md)).
- **Nothing was never an option.** If some things exist necessarily —
  [abstract objects](../mathematics/abstract-objects.md), say, or every
  consistent [mathematical structure](../mathematics/mathematical-universe-hypothesis.md)
  — then the empty world is not a possible world and the question lapses.
- **Nothing is improbable.** Nozick and others: there are many ways to be
  something and only one way to be nothing, so if worlds are equiprobable,
  something is overwhelmingly likely. The objection is that a measure over
  possible worlds is exactly what nobody has.
- **The question is malformed.** Explanation is a relation between facts within
  a world; asking for an explanation *of* the totality misuses the notion.
  [Kant](../lineage/kant.md)'s antinomies are the systematic version, and
  [Heidegger](../being/ontological-difference.md) turns the question round —
  its value is that it puts *us* in question, not that it wants an answer of
  this form.
- **Brute fact.** Accept it and deny the PSR. The cheapest reply, and the
  hardest to argue against, since the PSR has never had a proof.

Note the structural echo: this is the
[primacy dilemma](../aristotle/primacy-dilemma.md)'s problem shape — a demand
that the terminus of explanation be both inside and outside the system it
explains.
