The claim escalates. God has no *physical* parts (uncontroversial), no
*metaphysical* parts — no distinction between substance and accident, or between
God and God's nature, or between
[essence and existence](essence-existence-distinction.md) — and no distinction
among the attributes: God's power *is* God's knowledge *is* God's goodness *is*
God.

## Why it is held

- **From [pure actuality](pure-actuality.md).** Composition implies potentiality
  for the parts to be otherwise, and a purely actual being has none.
- **From aseity.** A composite depends on its parts and on whatever unites them,
  and the first principle depends on nothing.
- **From [the One](the-one.md).** The Neoplatonic route: the source of all unity
  cannot be composite. Christian and Islamic versions of the doctrine draw on
  this while refusing the further step of placing the first principle *beyond*
  being; God is *ipsum esse*, not above being.

## The objections

- **Attribute collapse.** If omnipotence and goodness are the same property,
  they should be intersubstitutable, and they are not.
- **Modal collapse.** If God is identical with His act of will, and God is
  necessary, then His act of will is necessary, and everything He wills is
  necessary — creation included. This is
  [Spinoza](../lineage/spinoza.md)'s conclusion cheerfully embraced, and the
  classical theist's nightmare; the standard reply distinguishes the necessity
  of the act from the contingency of its effect.
- **Intelligibility.** A thing identical with its own properties is not obviously
  a coherent notion.

Defenders (Stump, Kretzmann, Brower) answer that "identity" here is not the
modern logician's relation and that the doctrine is best read as a set of
denials — apophatic, not a positive description. Which returns it to
[Plotinus](../lineage/plotinus.md)'s territory.
