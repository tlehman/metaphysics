[Leibniz](../lineage/leibniz.md)'s formulation: nothing is without a reason, or
there is no effect without a cause. He treats it as coordinate with
[non-contradiction](../aristotle/principle-of-non-contradiction.md) — the two
principles on which all reasoning rests, the first governing necessary truths,
the second contingent ones.

Where the PNC is defended as *indubitable* rather than provable
(Aristotle's [strategy](../aristotle/principle-of-non-contradiction.md)), the
PSR has no comparable defence, and this is its permanent vulnerability. It is
also not obviously *one* principle: reasons may be causes, grounds, or purposes.

## What it buys

Almost everything in rationalist metaphysics is downstream:

- **[Cosmological argument](cosmological-argument.md).** The contingent series
  has a reason; that reason cannot be contingent; so there is a necessary being.
- **Identity of indiscernibles.** Two indiscernible things would leave God's
  choice of which is which without a reason.
- **Best of all possible worlds.** Creation requires a sufficient reason for
  *this* world.

## What it costs

Van Inwagen's argument makes the price explicit. Let *C* be the conjunction of
all contingent truths. By the PSR, *C* has an explanation *E*. If *E* is
contingent, *E* is a conjunct of *C*, so *C* explains itself — circular. If *E*
is necessary, then *C* follows necessarily from a necessary truth, so *C* is
necessary, and nothing is contingent. **Necessitarianism** is the result, which
[Spinoza](../lineage/spinoza.md) embraced and Leibniz spent enormous effort
avoiding via his distinction between what is necessary and what is merely
"inclined without necessitating."

The modern echo is exact: the
[mathematical universe hypothesis](../mathematics/mathematical-universe-hypothesis.md)
makes existence follow from consistency, which is necessitarianism reached by a
different road.
