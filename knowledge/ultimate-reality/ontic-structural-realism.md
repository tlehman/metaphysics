Worrall's *epistemic* structural realism came first, as a way to have both
sides of the scientific-realism dispute: the pessimistic meta-induction is
right that theoretical *ontologies* are discarded, but the no-miracles argument
is right that something is retained — and what is retained is mathematical
structure (Fresnel's equations survive the abandonment of the ether). So we know
the structure of the unobservable, not its nature.

Ladyman's *ontic* version drops the epistemic hedge: there is no hidden nature
we are ignorant of. Structure is what there is.

## The physics driving it

- **Quantum statistics.** Identical particles are not individuals in the
  classical sense; permutation invariance means "which particle is which" has no
  answer, so the individuals were never doing the work.
- **Entanglement.** The state of a whole is not determined by the states of its
  parts, which sits badly with parts as the fundamental items — and well with
  [priority monism](../being/substance-monism.md).
- **Gauge symmetry and general covariance.** The physical content is what is
  invariant, i.e. the structure.

## The metaphysical stakes

"Relations without relata" is the hard version, and the standard objection is
that it is incoherent — a relation needs something to relate. Moderate versions
keep objects but deny them intrinsic natures beyond their relational profile,
which is exactly *ante rem*
[structuralism](../mathematics/structuralism.md) applied to physics.

Follow the thought one step further and structure is not just what physics
describes but what reality *is* — the
[mathematical universe hypothesis](../mathematics/mathematical-universe-hypothesis.md).
Follow it back and it is the exact negation of
[Aristotle's substance](../aristotle/substance.md): what is fundamental is the
web of relations, and the independent *tode ti* is what turns out to be
derivative. This is the arc's contemporary terminus, and it names a
[fundamental](../being/fundamentality.md) reality with no theology in it.
