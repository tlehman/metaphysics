The *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781/1787) does not refute rationalist metaphysics
piecemeal; it argues that the whole enterprise misunderstands what the categories
are for. Concepts like substance and cause are conditions of the possibility of
*experience*, valid only of appearances. Applied beyond experience — to the soul,
the world as a whole, or God — they generate paralogisms, antinomies and an ideal
of reason without objects.

Three consequences that recur through this bundle:

- **"Existence is not a real predicate."** The attack on the ontological
  argument, and the ancestor of the Frege–Russell view of
  [existence](../being/existence.md) as second-order.
- **The antinomies.** Reason produces equally good arguments on both sides of
  cosmological questions, which for Kant shows the questions are ill-formed
  rather than hard — the systematic form of the "dissolution" reply to
  [why anything at all](../ultimate-reality/why-anything-at-all.md).
- **Arithmetic is synthetic *a priori*,** grounded in the pure intuition of
  time. This is precisely the claim [Frege](frege.md)'s
  [logicism](../mathematics/logicism.md) was built to refute, and precisely the
  claim Brouwer's [intuitionism](../mathematics/intuitionism.md) revives.

Kant's own metaphysics survives as *transcendental* — an account of the
conditions of cognition rather than of being — which
[Heidegger](heidegger.md) reads as still not asking the question and later
analytic metaphysics simply sets aside.
