Two great principles: [non-contradiction](../aristotle/principle-of-non-contradiction.md),
governing necessary truths, and the
[principle of sufficient reason](../ultimate-reality/principle-of-sufficient-reason.md),
governing contingent ones. From the second comes the question that names the
third arc's central problem — "[why is there something rather than
nothing?](../ultimate-reality/why-anything-at-all.md)" — and the answer that a
sufficient reason for the whole contingent series must lie outside it.

The system's other commitments are mostly damage control against
[Spinoza](spinoza.md). If the PSR is unrestricted, everything looks necessary;
Leibniz's escape is that God's choice of the best world is *inclined without
being necessitated*, so the world is contingent though there is a reason for it.
Whether that distinction survives scrutiny is the standing question.

Also his, and relevant here:

- **Monads** — simple, windowless, indivisible substances, each expressing the
  whole universe from a point of view. A radical answer to what a
  [substance](../aristotle/substance.md) is: no matter, no parts, no interaction.
- **The identity of indiscernibles**, argued from the PSR.
- **Truth as concept-containment**, which brings essence and necessary
  connection back together — compare
  [essence and modality](../being/essence-and-modality.md).
