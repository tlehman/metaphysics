*Sein und Zeit* (1927) begins from the claim that the question of the meaning of
being has been forgotten, and that the forgetting is not an oversight but the
shape of the tradition itself. See
[the ontological difference](../being/ontological-difference.md).

Heidegger's relation to [Aristotle](aristotle.md) is not dismissal — he lectured
on him for years and took *ousia*, *energeia* and *alêtheia* as the words in
which the tradition's decisions were made. The claim is that Aristotle
identified being with presence, and that
[first philosophy](../aristotle/first-philosophy.md)'s doubling as theology
([unmoved mover](../aristotle/unmoved-mover.md)) is where metaphysics becomes
onto-theology: the question of being answered by naming the highest being.

His positive route is to ask after being from the entity for whom being is an
issue — Dasein, whose analytic occupies most of *Sein und Zeit* — and after the
*Kehre*, from being's own historical disclosure rather than from any subject.

The contrast with the analytic treatment is instructive. Both
[Quine](quine.md) and Heidegger think traditional ontology is confused; Quine's
remedy is regimentation ([ontological
commitment](../being/ontological-commitment.md)), which Heidegger would regard
as the disease in its most advanced form.
