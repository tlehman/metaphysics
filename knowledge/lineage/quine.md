"On What There Is" (1948) supplies the criterion of
[ontological commitment](../being/ontological-commitment.md); "Two Dogmas of
Empiricism" (1951) supplies the holism; *Word and Object* (1960) supplies the
naturalism. Together they revived analytic metaphysics by making ontological
questions answerable — you settle them by regimenting your best theory and
reading off the quantifiers.

The unintended consequence is the [indispensability
argument](../mathematics/indispensability-argument.md): Quine, an avowed
nominalist by temperament, concluded that we are committed to sets because
science cannot do without them. That single argument is now the strongest case
for [mathematical platonism](../mathematics/mathematical-platonism.md), and its
author regarded it as a reluctant concession.

What his framework leaves out is what the current revival has put back. Quinean
being is flat, univocal and all-or-nothing — no degrees, no priority, no
structure. [Grounding](../being/grounding.md) and
[fundamentality](../being/fundamentality.md) reintroduce exactly the structure
[Aristotle](../aristotle/pros-hen-equivocity.md) built into "being" from the
start, and Fine's argument for essence over
[modality](../being/essence-and-modality.md) undoes the other half of Quine's
legacy.
