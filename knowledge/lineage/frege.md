The *Begriffsschrift* (1879) gives quantification and the predicate calculus; the
*Grundlagen* (1884) and *Grundgesetze* (1893/1903) put them to work on
[logicism](../mathematics/logicism.md), against
[Kant](kant.md)'s claim that arithmetic is synthetic *a priori*.

Method and results that outlast the programme:

- **The context principle** — never ask for the meaning of a word in isolation,
  only in the context of a sentence. This is what licenses defining numbers by
  fixing the truth conditions of identity statements about them, which is the
  [way-of-abstraction](../mathematics/abstract-objects.md).
- **Hume's Principle** and Frege's Theorem: second-order logic plus HP yields
  the Peano axioms. Recovered by neo-logicism after Basic Law V's collapse.
- **Sense and reference**, and the argument that
  [existence](../being/existence.md) is a second-order concept — "numbers exist"
  says a concept is instantiated.
- **Anti-psychologism.** Thoughts and numbers are objective without being
  physical, which is a defence of
  [abstract objects](../mathematics/abstract-objects.md) and makes Frege a
  [platonist](../mathematics/mathematical-platonism.md) about them — inheriting
  the epistemological debt [Benacerraf](../mathematics/benacerrafs-dilemma.md)
  would come to collect.

[Russell](russell.md)'s 1902 letter showed Basic Law V inconsistent — see
[Russell's paradox](../mathematics/russells-paradox.md). Frege's appendix to the
second *Grundgesetze* volume is one of the more honest documents in the history
of philosophy: rather than patch quietly, he set out the three questions the
contradiction raises — whether one may always speak of the extension of a
concept, how the exceptions are to be recognised, and whether coincidence of
extensions still licenses the inference it was meant to. His own proposed "way
out" was unsuccessful.
