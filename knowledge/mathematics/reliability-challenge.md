[Benacerraf's dilemma](benacerrafs-dilemma.md) originally leaned on the causal
theory of knowledge, and platonists waited for that theory to fall. It fell, and
the objection survived, because Field restated it without any causal premise at
all. The reliability of mathematicians is a striking empirical fact:

$$\text{For (almost) all } \varphi:\quad \text{mathematicians accept } \varphi \;\longrightarrow\; \varphi \text{ is true}$$

The challenge is not to justify any particular belief. It is to explain the
correlation itself.

1. Mathematicians are reliable: what they accept is, overwhelmingly, true.
2. This reliability must be explainable in principle. A correlation that could
   not even in principle be explained is not something we may simply help
   ourselves to; discovering that one of our belief-forming processes has that
   character undermines it.
3. [Platonism](mathematical-platonism.md) makes the reliability inexplicable,
   because [abstract objects](abstract-objects.md) are causally inert and
   [independent](mathematical-platonism.md) of us — so nothing about them
   shaped our beliefs, and nothing about our beliefs shaped them.
4. Therefore platonism undermines mathematical knowledge.

## Why premise 2 is where the work is

The platonist's best line is to attack the demand rather than the facts. If
mathematical truths are *necessary*, there is no possible world where our
methods deliver falsehoods, so what exactly is left to explain? The reply — due
to Field and sharpened since — is that necessity does not by itself dissolve a
reliability question: we would still want to know why *these* creatures, with
*this* history, converge on the truths rather than on some consistent
alternative. Compare a reliable barometer, which is necessary given the physics
but still has a mechanism.

## The available answers

Each is a live position rather than a patch:

- **Deny (3) by enlarging the realm.**
  [Plenitudinous platonism](plenitudinous-platonism.md): if every consistent
  theory is true of something, there is no targeting left to explain, and
  reliability reduces to our tendency to be consistent.
- **Deny (3) by shrinking the objects.** Make them
  [structures](structuralism.md) or
  [potential](mathematical-potentialism.md) rather than a fixed independent
  population, so the truths are settled by facts closer to home.
- **Deny (1) or (3) by changing the subject.**
  [Fictionalism](fictionalism.md) denies the beliefs are true;
  [nominalism](mathematical-nominalism.md) denies there is anything to track.
- **Accept the demand and pay it.** Gödel's mathematical intuition — a
  non-sensory faculty analogous to perception — plus the abductive claim that
  axioms are confirmed by their fruitfulness, as physical hypotheses are. Few
  find the faculty credible; more find the abductive half serious.
- **Deny (2).** Argue that reliability with respect to necessary truths needs no
  explanation of the demanded kind. The most common platonist move, and the one
  the debate now turns on.

The same schema, with "numbers" replaced, is the standard access objection to
transcendent [universals](../being/realism-about-universals.md), to
[Plato's Forms](../ultimate-reality/theory-of-forms.md), and to moral realism —
it is a general problem about knowing a realm that cannot answer back.
