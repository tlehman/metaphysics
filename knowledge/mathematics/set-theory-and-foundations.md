Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with Choice is the default answer to "what is
mathematics built from?": nearly every classical object can be coded as a set,
and nearly every classical theorem proved from the ZFC axioms. That gives
mathematics one currency, and gives ontology one target — if numbers are sets,
the [platonism](mathematical-platonism.md) question is a question about sets.

## The independence problem

Gödel (1938) showed the continuum hypothesis is consistent with ZFC; Cohen
(1963), by forcing, that its negation is too. So CH is *independent*: the axioms
we have do not decide how many real numbers there are.

Three metaphysical readings, and the choice among them is not mathematical:

- **Universe view.** There is one intended cumulative hierarchy *V*, CH has a
  determinate truth value, and our axioms are incomplete. The programme is then
  to find new axioms — large cardinals, forcing axioms, *V* = Ultimate *L* — that
  settle it on independent grounds. Gödel's own view.
- **Multiverse view (Hamkins).** There are many equally legitimate set-theoretic
  universes; CH is settled differently in different ones and has no univocal
  answer, the way the parallel postulate has none.
- **Formalist/pluralist deflation.** The question was never about anything —
  see [formalism](formalism.md).

The [identification problem](structuralism.md) sits underneath all this: even
granting one universe of sets, nothing in it makes 2 the Zermelo numeral rather
than the von Neumann one.

The reason foundations belong in a metaphysics bundle is that "which sets exist"
is a [fundamentality](../being/fundamentality.md) question in mathematical
clothing, and the answer decides what
[abstract objects](abstract-objects.md) there are.
