# mathematics

The philosophy of mathematics as a metaphysical question — one of Aristotle's own aporiai. Ontology, epistemology and application, and the positions that satisfy any two of the three by abandoning the third.

## Concepts

* [Abstract Objects](abstract-objects.md) - Objects that are non-spatiotemporal and causally inert — numbers, sets, propositions, types — whose admission is the dividing line between platonism and nominalism, and whose causal isolation is what makes knowledge of them a problem.
* [Benacerraf's Dilemma](benacerrafs-dilemma.md) - A uniform semantics for mathematical and ordinary language pushes toward platonism; a workable epistemology pushes away from it — and no account has managed both, which is the organising problem of the whole field.
* [Mathematical Fictionalism](fictionalism.md) - That mathematical sentences are systematically false but useful — true only in the way "Holmes lived on Baker Street" is true — so mathematics is a fiction we reason inside rather than a body of truths about anything.
* [Formalism](formalism.md) - That mathematics is the manipulation of symbols under rules, with no subject matter beyond the game itself — refined by Hilbert into a programme for proving the consistency of infinitary mathematics by finitary means, which Gödel showed cannot be carried out.
* [Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems](godel-incompleteness.md) - Any consistent, effectively axiomatised theory containing enough arithmetic has true sentences it cannot prove, and cannot prove its own consistency — which ended Hilbert's programme and is routinely over-read into claims about minds and machines.
* [The Indispensability Argument](indispensability-argument.md) - Quine and Putnam's argument that since our best scientific theories quantify ineliminably over mathematical objects, and we should believe our best theories, we should believe in mathematical objects — the strongest case for platonism, and the one nominalists must dismantle.
* [Intuitionism](intuitionism.md) - Brouwer's view that mathematics is a mental construction, not a discovery — so a statement is true only when constructed, excluded middle fails for the infinite, and a large part of classical mathematics has to go.
* [Logicism](logicism.md) - Frege's programme of deriving arithmetic from logic plus definitions, so that numbers are logical objects and arithmetical truth is analytic — wrecked by Russell's paradox, partly recovered by neo-logicism via Hume's Principle.
* [Mathematical Nominalism](mathematical-nominalism.md) - That there are no mathematical objects — defended either by rewriting science without them (Field's programme) or by accepting the sentences while denying they are about anything (deflationary and modal strategies).
* [Mathematical Platonism](mathematical-platonism.md) - That mathematical objects exist, are abstract, and are independent of minds and language — the view that takes mathematical language at face value and pays for it in epistemology.
* [The Mathematical Universe Hypothesis](mathematical-universe-hypothesis.md) - Tegmark's claim that physical reality is not merely described by but *is* a mathematical structure, so every structure exists physically — the strongest possible answer to the application problem, at the cost of making the hypothesis nearly untestable.
* [Philosophy of Mathematics](philosophy-of-mathematics.md) - The hub of the mathematical arc — what mathematical objects are, how we could know anything about them, and why a subject with no obvious contact with the world describes it so well.
* [Set Theory and Foundations](set-theory-and-foundations.md) - ZFC as the standard foundation in which essentially all mathematics can be modelled, and the independence results — continuum hypothesis, large cardinals — that turn a technical question into a metaphysical one about whether there is a single universe of sets.
* [Mathematical Structuralism](structuralism.md) - That mathematics studies structures, not objects — 2 is a *position* in the natural-number structure with no properties beyond its structural ones, which dissolves the identification problem and makes "what is a number?" a question with no further answer.
* [The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Mathematics](unreasonable-effectiveness.md) - Wigner's observation that mathematics developed for internal reasons keeps turning out to describe physical reality with unearned precision — a "miracle" every position in the philosophy of mathematics owes an account of.
