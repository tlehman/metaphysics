Wigner, "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Mathematics in the Natural Sciences"
(1960): "the miracle of the appropriateness of the language of mathematics for
the formulation of the laws of physics is a wonderful gift which we neither
understand nor deserve."

The force of the puzzle is in the cases where the mathematics came *first*, for
reasons internal to mathematics, and the physics arrived afterwards to claim it:
conic sections and planetary orbits; Riemannian geometry and general relativity;
group representation theory and the classification of particles; complex Hilbert
spaces and quantum mechanics.

## The available answers

| Answer | Says |
|---|---|
| Selection effect | we notice the fits and forget the vast unused remainder; physics keeps only the mathematics that worked |
| Evolutionary/pragmatic | mathematics was abstracted from the world in the first place, so its returning to fit is no miracle — the [Aristotelian](../aristotle/mathematical-abstractionism.md) answer |
| Structural | science tracks structure, and mathematics is the science of structure — see [ontic structural realism](../ultimate-reality/ontic-structural-realism.md) |
| Deflationary | mathematics is a flexible language; anything expressible is expressible in it, so "fit" is cheap |
| Maximal | reality *is* mathematical, so the fit is identity — the [mathematical universe hypothesis](mathematical-universe-hypothesis.md) |

This is the third of the [three organising
questions](philosophy-of-mathematics.md), and it discriminates between positions
sharply. [Platonism](mathematical-platonism.md) makes the fit between two
independent realms mysterious; [fictionalism](fictionalism.md) must explain how
a falsehood is so useful (Field's conservativeness);
[intuitionism](intuitionism.md), making mathematics a mental construction, has
the hardest version of the problem.

The [indispensability argument](indispensability-argument.md) is the same
observation turned into a premise rather than a puzzle.
