Three positions travel under the name.

- **Game formalism.** Mathematics is a game with marks on paper; "2+2=4" is no
  more about anything than a legal chess move is. Frege's objection is
  standing: a game does not get *applied*, and applicability is precisely what
  needs explaining — see [unreasonable
  effectiveness](unreasonable-effectiveness.md).
- **Term formalism.** Numerals *are* the numbers; mathematics is about its own
  syntax. Trades abstract numbers for abstract types, so it does not escape
  [abstract objects](abstract-objects.md) as cheaply as it looks.
- **Hilbert's programme.** Much the most serious. Divide mathematics into a
  *contentful finitary* core — statements about concrete finite configurations,
  which are meaningful — and an *ideal* infinitary part, which is a useful
  instrument with no content of its own. Then use only finitary means to prove
  that the ideal part is consistent, and hence a conservative extension that
  never yields a false finitary conclusion.

Hilbert's aim was to keep Cantor's paradise without paying the ontological
price — an instrumentalism about the infinite that is structurally the same move
as Field's [conservativeness](mathematical-nominalism.md) claim, and Aristotle's
insistence that infinity is only ever potential.

[Gödel's second incompleteness theorem](godel-incompleteness.md) shows no
consistent system that contains enough arithmetic can prove its own consistency,
so the finitary core cannot certify the ideal part. Gentzen's consistency proof
for arithmetic goes through, but at the cost of transfinite induction up to
ε₀ — not finitary in Hilbert's sense.
