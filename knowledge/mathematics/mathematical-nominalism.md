The mathematical face of [nominalism](../being/nominalism.md). Its motivation is
almost entirely epistemological: if there were no
[abstract objects](abstract-objects.md), there would be no
[access problem](benacerrafs-dilemma.md) to solve.

The obstacle is equally clear — the
[indispensability argument](indispensability-argument.md). Nominalist programmes
divide by which premise they attack.

## Hard-road nominalism

**Field**, *Science Without Numbers* (1980). Attack indispensability directly by
doing the paraphrase. Two claims:

- **Nominalisation.** Newtonian gravitation can be reformulated with
  quantification only over spacetime points and regions, using Hilbert-style
  synthetic geometry — no numbers.
- **Conservativeness.** Mathematics need not be *true* to be *useful*: adding a
  mathematical theory to a nominalistic one proves no new nominalistic
  conclusions. Mathematics is a conservative extension, a device for shortening
  derivations.

Cost: the programme has not been extended convincingly to quantum mechanics, and
critics doubt spacetime regions are nominalistically respectable.

## Easy-road nominalism

Concede that mathematics cannot be paraphrased away, and deny that using a
theory commits you to its truth. Yablo's figuralism, Melia's weaselling, Azzouni
on the difference between quantification and ontological commitment. The cost is
owed at the other end: an account of how a *false* theory explains.

Modal structuralism (Hellman) takes a third route — read mathematical claims as
claims about what would hold in *any* structure of the right type, trading
objects for modality. See [structuralism](structuralism.md).

The nearest ancient relative is
[Aristotle's abstractionism](../aristotle/mathematical-abstractionism.md), which
gets nominalist results without paraphrase by relocating mathematical objects
inside perceptible magnitudes.
