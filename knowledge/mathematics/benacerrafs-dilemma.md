From "Mathematical Truth" (1973). Benacerraf sets two desiderata against each
other and observes that every position on offer satisfies one by abandoning the
other.

**Desideratum 1 — semantic uniformity.** "There are three large cities older
than New York" and "there are three perfect numbers greater than 17" should get
the same semantic treatment. The obvious one makes both existentially
quantified claims about objects, so the second is about numbers, and if it is
true there are numbers. → [platonism](mathematical-platonism.md).

**Desideratum 2 — a workable epistemology.** Knowledge requires some appropriate
relation between the knower and what makes the claim true. On the causal theory
Benacerraf assumed, that relation is causal. But
[abstract objects](abstract-objects.md) are causally inert. → not platonism.

## Why it survived the causal theory of knowledge

The causal theory has few adherents now, and platonists initially took that as
a reprieve. Field's reformulation removed it: forget causation, and just ask the
platonist to explain the *reliability* of mathematicians — the fact that when
mathematicians accept *p*, *p* is true. Any belief-forming process whose
reliability is in principle inexplicable is thereby undermined, and no story
seems available for objects that could not have influenced us in any way. That
restatement is now the live form of the objection; it is set out in full at
[the reliability challenge](reliability-challenge.md).

Benacerraf's *other* objection is metaphysical rather than epistemological and
is filed with [structuralism](structuralism.md): numbers appear to have only
structural properties, and nothing can have only those.

## How each position pays

| Position | Keeps | Pays |
|---|---|---|
| [Platonism](mathematical-platonism.md) | semantics | owes reliability |
| [Nominalism](mathematical-nominalism.md) | epistemology | owes paraphrase or an account of useful falsehood |
| [Fictionalism](fictionalism.md) | semantics, formally | denies truth outright |
| [Structuralism](structuralism.md), *ante rem* | semantics | inherits the platonist debt |
| [Intuitionism](intuitionism.md) | epistemology | revises logic and abandons classical mathematics |
| [Plenitudinous platonism](plenitudinous-platonism.md) | both, formally | makes truth cheap and determinacy unavailable |
| [Potentialism](mathematical-potentialism.md) | both, formally | takes on primitive modality and a never-completed hierarchy |

The dilemma is the ancestor of the access problems raised elsewhere in this
bundle: against transcendent
[universals](../being/realism-about-universals.md) and against
[Plato's Forms](../ultimate-reality/theory-of-forms.md), which face the same
question with the same structure.
