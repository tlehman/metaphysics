Tegmark's argument starts from a premise about external reality: if a reality
exists independently of us, its description must be free of human baggage — free
of concepts whose content depends on our language or biology. A description with
no such baggage is a description purely of relations among elements with no
intrinsic properties. That is exactly what a mathematical structure is.
Therefore, he argues, external reality *is* a mathematical structure.

The corollary is a Level IV multiverse: mathematical existence and physical
existence are the same, so every consistent structure is physically real, and
ours has no special status beyond being one we can be in.

## Why it belongs on both arcs

It is the terminal position on the mathematical arc, giving the maximal answer
to [unreasonable effectiveness](unreasonable-effectiveness.md) — the fit is
identity, so there is nothing left to explain. And it is a claim about
[ultimate reality](../ultimate-reality/ultimate-explanation.md): it answers
"[why is there something rather than
nothing?](../ultimate-reality/why-anything-at-all.md)" by making existence
follow from consistency, so that the question does not arise. That is a
Spinozist move in modern dress — everything necessary, nothing contingent.

## Objections

- **Gödelian.** [Incompleteness](godel-incompleteness.md) makes "the set of all
  mathematical structures" formally awkward; Tegmark's response is to restrict
  to computable structures (the "Computable Universe Hypothesis"), which is a
  substantial retreat.
- **Measure.** With every structure real, predicting anything requires a measure
  over structures, and nobody has one.
- **Category mistake.** Structuralism says mathematical objects have no
  properties beyond structural ones; physical things seem to have more —
  causal powers, phenomenal character. See
  [ontic structural realism](../ultimate-reality/ontic-structural-realism.md)
  for the milder version of the same thought, and
  [Aristotle's abstractionism](../aristotle/mathematical-abstractionism.md) for
  the exact inversion: numbers depend on substances, not substances on numbers.
