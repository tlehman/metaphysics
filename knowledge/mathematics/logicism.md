[Frege](../lineage/frege.md)'s target was
[Kant](../lineage/kant.md)'s claim that arithmetic is synthetic *a priori*,
grounded in the pure intuition of time. If arithmetic follows from logic and
definitions alone, it is analytic, and no faculty of intuition is needed — which
would have been a clean answer to what became
[Benacerraf's epistemological horn](benacerrafs-dilemma.md).

The apparatus: define number in purely logical terms (the number of *F*s is the
extension of the concept *equinumerous with F*), prove the Peano axioms from
that. Frege got it done in *Grundgesetze*, and then Russell's letter of 1902
showed that Basic Law V — the extensions of *F* and *G* are the same iff exactly
the *F*s are the *G*s — yields a contradiction. Basic Law V entails
[naive comprehension](naive-comprehension.md), and one instance of that is
[Russell's paradox](russells-paradox.md).

## What survived

- **Russell and Whitehead's *Principia*.** Arithmetic from a
  [ramified type theory](type-theory.md), but needing the axioms of infinity and
  reducibility, which are not plausibly logical. Logicism reached, at best,
  set-theoreticism.
- **Neo-logicism (Wright, Hale).** Drop Basic Law V and take **Hume's
  Principle** as the foundation: the number of *F*s = the number of *G*s iff the
  *F*s and *G*s are in one-to-one correspondence. [Frege's Theorem](../references/sep-frege-theorem.md) — that
  second-order logic plus Hume's Principle yields the Peano axioms — is a real
  result, and it means [Russell's paradox](russells-paradox.md) never touched
  the derivation of arithmetic, only the law Frege chose to found it on;
  the dispute is whether Hume's Principle is analytic, or a covert existence
  postulate. This is the [way-of-abstraction](abstract-objects.md) conception of
  abstract objects at work.

Logicism is one of the three foundational programmes, alongside
[formalism](formalism.md) and [intuitionism](intuitionism.md), whose
limits [Gödel](godel-incompleteness.md) fixed.
