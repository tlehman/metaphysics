Colyvan's canonical statement:

1. We ought to have ontological commitment to all and only those entities that
   are indispensable to our best scientific theories.
2. Mathematical entities are indispensable to our best scientific theories.
3. Therefore we ought to have ontological commitment to mathematical entities.

Premise 1 packs in two Quinean doctrines. **Naturalism** — science, not first
philosophy, is the arbiter of what there is. **Confirmational holism** — theories
face the tribunal of experience as wholes, so the mathematics used in a
confirmed theory is confirmed along with it. The criterion that turns
"indispensable" into "committed" is
[ontological commitment](../being/ontological-commitment.md).

It is the only argument for [platonism](mathematical-platonism.md) that does not
appeal to mathematical intuition, which is why it matters: it makes belief in
numbers continuous with belief in electrons.

## Attacks

- **Deny premise 2 — nominalise the science.** Field's programme; see
  [mathematical nominalism](mathematical-nominalism.md). Costly beyond
  Newtonian gravitation.
- **Deny holism.** Maddy: scientists do not in fact treat the mathematics as
  confirmed by the experiment, and idealisations (continuous fluids, infinite
  populations) are used freely without belief.
- **Deny that use is commitment.** Easy-road nominalism — Melia, Yablo, Azzouni.
- **Turn it around.** Sober: confirmation is contrastive, and mathematics is
  common to all competing hypotheses, so it is never tested.

## The enhanced version

Baker and Colyvan strengthen the argument by narrowing it: we should commit to
mathematical entities that play an *indispensable explanatory* role, not merely
a representational one. The stock case is the North American periodical cicada's
prime-numbered life cycle (13 and 17 years), where the explanation of why the
period is prime appears to be number-theoretic rather than biological. Whether
such explanations are genuinely mathematical is now the live front.
