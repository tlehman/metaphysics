An inventory is flat; reality, most metaphysicians think, is not. Tables exist
*and* are made of something; sets exist *and* are built from members. To be
fundamental is to be at the terminus — ungrounded, primitive, "God's-eye"
furniture.

Two ways to say it:

- **Absolute** — a thing (or fact, or property) is fundamental *simpliciter*.
  Usually paired with the assumption that [grounding](grounding.md) is
  well-founded: no infinite descent, no loops, so there is a bottom.
- **Relative** — fundamentality is only comparative, one thing more basic than
  another, with no requirement that anything be basic outright. This makes room
  for *metaphysical infinitism*, where every level is grounded in a lower one
  forever.

## The Aristotelian precedent

[Substance](../aristotle/substance.md) is the original fundamentality thesis:
substances are separable, everything else is
[in](../aristotle/categories.md) or [said of](../aristotle/categories.md) them,
and "if there were no primary substances, there could not be anything else"
(*Cat.* 2b3–6). Λ pushes further and asks whether the fundamental *level* is
itself unified — which is what makes [the unmoved
mover](../aristotle/unmoved-mover.md) a fundamentality claim rather than merely
a cosmological one.

Live disputes: whether the fundamental is the smallest
([microphysicalism](../ultimate-reality/ontic-structural-realism.md) and its
rivals) or the largest ([priority monism](substance-monism.md)); whether
structure rather than objects is fundamental; and whether the question
"[why is there something rather than
nothing?](../ultimate-reality/why-anything-at-all.md)" is a demand for the
grounds of the fundamental, and so incoherent.
