Also *abstract particulars*, *moments*, *individual accidents*. A trope is a
property instance taken as itself a particular: the redness *of this rose*, not
redness. Two red roses have two rednesses that exactly resemble.

The position claims both sides' benefits. Against
[realism](realism-about-universals.md), it avoids one entity wholly present in
many places. Against austere [nominalism](nominalism.md), it has real
properties in the world to do causal and explanatory work — this rose's redness,
not merely the predicate.

## What it buys

- **Resemblance without universals.** Exact-resemblance classes of tropes
  replace universals; the relation is primitive, which is the standard
  objection.
- **Bundle theories of substance.** A concrete particular is a bundle of
  compresent tropes. This is the sharpest break with
  [Aristotle](../aristotle/substance.md), for whom substance is the independent
  item and every accident is dependent on it — tropes invert the priority, and
  it is a genuine question whether the resulting bundle can be *separable* and a
  [tode ti](../glossary/tode-ti.md) at once, which is exactly what the
  [subject criterion](../aristotle/subject-criterion.md) demanded.
- **Truthmakers.** A trope is a ready-made truthmaker for a predication without
  the need for states of affairs.

Aristotle's own view of non-substantial particulars — the individual bit of a
shade that inheres in one substance and no other — is the historical ancestor,
and whether the [*Categories*](../aristotle/categories.md) really contains
such items is itself disputed.
