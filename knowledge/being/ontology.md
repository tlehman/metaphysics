Hub of the general-metaphysics arc. Where Aristotle asked what it is to be
*qua* being ([being qua being](../aristotle/being-qua-being.md)), the modern
discipline splits the question in two:

- **Ontology proper** — what entities are there? Numbers, properties, events,
  possibilia, holes?
- **Metaontology** — what are we doing when we ask, and what would settle it?

The second question is largely [Quine](../lineage/quine.md)'s doing:
[ontological commitment](ontological-commitment.md) turned "what exists?" into
a question about the bound variables of one's best regimented theory, which
made ontology tractable and, to critics, trivial.

Two further distinctions organise most of the terrain:

| Question | Contrast |
|---|---|
| What is there? | ontology, the inventory |
| What is fundamental? | [fundamentality](fundamentality.md) — the inventory is flat, reality is not |
| In virtue of what? | [grounding](grounding.md) — non-causal dependence |
| Repeatable or not? | [universals and particulars](universals-and-particulars.md) |

The revival of the last three is a deliberate return to a broadly Aristotelian
picture: it is not enough to list what exists, one must say which items hold the
others up — exactly the work Aristotle's [substance](../aristotle/substance.md)
was doing.
