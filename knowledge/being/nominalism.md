The word carries two distinct denials, and confusing them wastes arguments:

1. **No universals** — everything that exists is particular. The medieval
   dispute, against [realism](realism-about-universals.md).
2. **No abstract objects** — nothing exists outside space and time. The modern
   dispute, against [platonism](../mathematics/mathematical-platonism.md).

A trope theorist is a nominalist in sense (1) and need not be one in sense (2);
a mathematical structuralist may be the reverse.

## The varieties, and their costs

| Variety | Explains sharing by |
|---|---|
| Predicate nominalism | falling under the same predicate |
| Concept nominalism | falling under the same concept |
| Class nominalism | membership in the same class |
| Resemblance nominalism | resembling paradigm particulars |
| Mereological nominalism | being part of the same scattered whole |

The standing objection is that each takes as primitive what realism explains.
Class nominalism must handle coextensive but distinct properties (*renate* and
*cordate*) and uninstantiated ones; resemblance nominalism must handle
Goodman's companionship and imperfect-community difficulties, and must treat
resemblance as primitive rather than as grounded in shared nature.

Against this, nominalism claims a decisive advantage in parsimony, and in not
owing an account of how one thing is wholly present in many places at once.

Its mathematical form is [mathematical
nominalism](../mathematics/mathematical-nominalism.md), whose central obstacle
is the [indispensability
argument](../mathematics/indispensability-argument.md).
