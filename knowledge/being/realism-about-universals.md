If two things are both round, realism says there is one thing, *roundness*,
wholly present in each. This explains resemblance without reducing it, supports
laws of nature construed as relations among universals (Armstrong, Dretske,
Tooley), and gives predication a truthmaker.

## The two versions

- **Transcendent (*ante rem*, Platonic).** Universals exist whether or not
  anything instantiates them. See [the theory of
  Forms](../ultimate-reality/theory-of-forms.md). Buys necessity and
  independence; pays with the participation problem — what *is* it for a
  particular to partake of a Form? — and with a causal-access worry that
  reappears exactly as [Benacerraf's
  dilemma](../mathematics/benacerrafs-dilemma.md).
- **Immanent (*in rebus*, Aristotelian).** A universal exists only where it is
  instantiated. Aristotle's [categories](../aristotle/categories.md) have this
  shape: colour is *in* body, and could not be in body in general unless it were
  in some particular bodies. Buys naturalistic respectability; pays with the
  result that which universals exist is a contingent empirical matter, and with
  the awkwardness of "wholly present in many places at once."

Armstrong's *sparse* realism restricts universals to those the completed
sciences need, so that not every predicate names one — which blocks the
Bradley-style regresses and the paradoxes generated by abundant conceptions.

The alternatives are [nominalism](nominalism.md) and
[trope theory](trope-theory.md); the version of this fight that concerns numbers
is [mathematical platonism](../mathematics/mathematical-platonism.md).
