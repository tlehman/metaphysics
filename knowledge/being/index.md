# being

General ontology, ancient to contemporary: what there is, whether universals are among it, and the revival of Aristotelian priority as grounding and fundamentality.

## Concepts

* [Essence and Modality](essence-and-modality.md) - Whether essence reduces to de re necessity — Kit Fine's counterexamples say no, reviving a broadly Aristotelian notion of essence as definitional rather than modal, and with it the idea that metaphysical necessity is *explained* by essence.
* [Existence](existence.md) - Whether existence is a first-order property of individuals or, as the Frege–Russell orthodoxy has it, a second-order property expressed by the quantifier — the question that decides whether "there are things that do not exist" is even coherent.
* [Fundamentality](fundamentality.md) - The idea that reality has a layered structure with something at the bottom — what is not grounded in anything else — which turns "what is there?" into the sharper question of what the rest depends on.
* [Grounding](grounding.md) - Non-causal metaphysical determination — the relation reported by "in virtue of," which is meant to be strict-partial-ordering, explanatory and hyperintensional, and which contemporary metaphysics uses to do the work Aristotle assigned to the formal cause.
* [Nominalism](nominalism.md) - The denial that universals (or, in the other sense, that abstract objects) exist — everything is particular, and the work universals were hired for must be done by predicates, classes, resemblance, or paraphrase.
* [Ontological Commitment](ontological-commitment.md) - Quine's criterion — to be is to be the value of a bound variable — which makes ontology a question about the regimented form of one's best theory, and which supplies the engine of the indispensability argument.
* [The Ontological Difference](ontological-difference.md) - Heidegger's distinction between beings and the being of beings, and his charge that the whole tradition from Aristotle onward answered the question of being by naming a supreme *being* — a diagnosis aimed squarely at the identification of first philosophy with theology.
* [Ontology](ontology.md) - The inventory question — what there is — split off from the rest of metaphysics as its own discipline, and the modern heir of Aristotle's study of being qua being.
* [Realism About Universals](realism-about-universals.md) - The view that universals exist and are wholly present in each of their instances — Platonic if they exist uninstantiated, Aristotelian if they exist only in rebus, with the second version buying immanence at the cost of contingency.
* [Monism](substance-monism.md) - That there is fundamentally one thing — either one object at all (Parmenides, Spinoza) or, in Schaffer's priority version, one *basic* object, the cosmos, on which every proper part depends.
* [Trope Theory](trope-theory.md) - Properties are particulars — this rose's redness is not numerically identical with that one's — so resemblance classes of tropes do the work of universals, and substances can be built out of bundles of them rather than presupposed.
* [Universals and Particulars](universals-and-particulars.md) - The distinction between what can be wholly present in many things at once and what cannot — Aristotle's katholou/kath' hekaston, and the axis along which realism, nominalism and trope theory divide.
