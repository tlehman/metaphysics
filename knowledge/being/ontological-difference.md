[Heidegger](../lineage/heidegger.md)'s claim is not that the tradition answered
the question of being badly but that it stopped asking it. *Sein und Zeit*
(1927) opens by insisting the question has been forgotten, and that the
forgetting is systematic: asked what being is, metaphysics reliably answers by
pointing at some *entity* — a highest, most real, most causally originating one.

That is what he calls **onto-theology**. The structure is visible in
[Aristotle](../aristotle/first-philosophy.md) without distortion: first
philosophy is both the universal science of [being qua
being](../aristotle/being-qua-being.md) *and* theology, the science of one
eternal separate substance — and Aristotle explicitly says the second grounds
the first (Ε.1, 1026a29–32). The identification Heidegger objects to is the one
Aristotle's own interpreters find hardest, so the charge lands on a real seam.

## The difference itself

Being (*Sein*) is not a being (*Seiendes*), not the highest genus, and not any
item in an inventory. To treat it as one is precisely to lose it. Heidegger's
positive method is therefore not to add an entity but to ask after the
*meaning* of being from the side of the entity for whom being is an issue —
Dasein — and later, after the *Kehre*, from the side of being's own historical
self-disclosure.

Read charitably, Aristotle has a partial defence: [pros hen
equivocity](../aristotle/pros-hen-equivocity.md) is itself a denial that being
is a genus, and Λ's god unifies being "with reference to one thing" rather than
by being the biggest thing on a list. Whether that is a different structure from
onto-theology or its purest instance is the whole dispute.

Adjacent critiques: [Kant](../lineage/kant.md)'s, which limits metaphysics from
the side of cognition rather than history, and Carnap's, which treats the
question as a pseudo-question — see
[ontological commitment](ontological-commitment.md).
