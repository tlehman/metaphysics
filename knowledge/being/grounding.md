A set exists in virtue of its members; a disjunction is true in virtue of its
true disjunct; the mental holds in virtue of the physical, if physicalism is
true. None of these is causation, none is mere supervenience, and none is
identity. Grounding is the attempt to take the "in virtue of" seriously as a
single relation worth theorising.

## The standard profile

- **Strict partial order** — irreflexive, asymmetric, transitive (each has been
  contested).
- **Hyperintensional** — necessary equivalents can differ in what they ground,
  which is exactly why supervenience was inadequate.
- **Explanatory** — grounding either *is* a form of metaphysical explanation or
  backs one.
- **Well-founded, on the usual assumption** — chains terminate in something
  ungrounded, which is [fundamentality](fundamentality.md).

## The Aristotelian debt

This is openly a recovery. Aristotle's priority relations — non-substances
depend on [substances](../aristotle/substance.md);
[form is the cause of a compound's being](../aristotle/substantial-form.md) what
it is; species predications are explained by underlying form predications — are
grounding claims in all but name, and Ζ.17's insistence that "what is being
looked for is the cause in virtue of which the matter is something" is the
slogan. Aristotle's [four causes](../aristotle/four-causes.md) already
distinguished several senses of explanatory priority; grounding keeps the
non-causal ones.

Sceptics (Wilson, Koslicki, Daly) reply that "grounding" runs together many
specific relations — set membership, constitution, realisation, determinable/
determinate — with nothing general left over, and that the small relations were
doing fine on their own.

Related: [essence](essence-and-modality.md) as the other half of the revival,
and [priority monism](substance-monism.md) as the most dramatic thing one can
say with it.
