The received view descends from [Frege](../lineage/frege.md) and Russell:
existence is not a property of individuals but a second-order property, what is
expressed by the existential quantifier. "Horses exist" says the concept *horse*
is instantiated; "Pegasus does not exist" says the concept *Pegasus* is not.
The view dissolves the old puzzle of negative existentials — how to say truly of
a thing that it is not — by denying that such statements are about a thing at
all. [Kant](../lineage/kant.md)'s "existence is not a real predicate," aimed at
the ontological argument, is the ancestor.

## The live disagreements

- **Meinongianism.** Some things do not exist but are still available for
  quantification and reference. Distinguishes *being* from *existence* in a way
  the Frege–Russell view forbids.
- **First-order views.** Existence *is* a property of individuals, just a
  universal one — which is compatible with quantifying over more than what
  exists.
- **Whether existence comes in degrees or kinds.** The Aristotelian answer is
  emphatically yes: substances are beings in the primary sense and everything
  else derivatively ([pros hen equivocity](../aristotle/pros-hen-equivocity.md)).
  Contemporary metaphysics mostly rejects degrees of being while reintroducing
  the same structure as [fundamentality](fundamentality.md).

The methodological question — how the debate is settled — is
[ontological commitment](ontological-commitment.md).
