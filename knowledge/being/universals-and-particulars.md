A universal is what is "said of many" ([katholou](../glossary/katholou.md)); a
particular ([kath' hekaston](../glossary/kath-hekaston.md)) is unrepeatable and
predicated of nothing. Two distinct grains of sand are two particulars; if they
share a shade of colour, either something numerically one is in both, or
something must be said instead.

Aristotle keeps this apart from a second distinction that is easy to conflate
with it: a [tode ti](../glossary/tode-ti.md) is a *fully determinate* thing, not
further differentiable. Determinacy and unrepeatability come apart, which leaves
room for a "universal *tode ti*" — the manoeuvre that rescues
[substantial form](../aristotle/substantial-form.md) from
[Ζ.13](../aristotle/universals-and-substance.md).

## The three answers

| Position | Says |
|---|---|
| [Realism](realism-about-universals.md) | universals exist, numerically one across instances |
| [Nominalism](nominalism.md) | only particulars exist; resemblance or predicate-application does the work |
| [Trope theory](trope-theory.md) | properties are particular; resemblance classes of tropes do the work of universals |

The medieval debate ran the same three ways and named the middle position
*conceptualism*. When the argument moves to numbers it becomes the debate over
[abstract objects](../mathematics/abstract-objects.md) and
[mathematical platonism](../mathematics/mathematical-platonism.md).
