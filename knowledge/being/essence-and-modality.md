The twentieth-century orthodoxy, after Quine's attack and Kripke's rehabilitation
of *de re* modality, was that an essential property is one a thing could not
have lacked while existing. Essence, so understood, is a modal notion, and
possible-worlds semantics handles the whole subject.

Fine's *Essence and Modality* (1994) breaks the reduction with counterexamples
running the same way each time:

- Socrates is necessarily a member of {Socrates}. But membership in that set is
  no part of what Socrates *is*; the set's essence involves Socrates, not
  conversely.
- Socrates is necessarily distinct from the Eiffel Tower, and necessarily such
  that 2+2=4. Neither belongs to his nature.

The modal criterion is thus too coarse. It is symmetric where essence is
asymmetric, and it sweeps in every necessary truth whatever.

## The Aristotelian recovery

Fine's proposal is that essence is prior: an essential truth about *x* is one
true "in virtue of the identity of *x*," and metaphysical necessity is what
follows from the essences of all things. That restores
[Aristotle's picture](../aristotle/essence.md) almost exactly — essence as what
a real definition states, [kath' hauto](../glossary/kath-hauto.md) predication,
definition of *things not words* — while dropping the biology and the
[hylomorphism](../aristotle/hylomorphism.md).

It also makes essence a species of explanation, which is why the same revival
produced [grounding](grounding.md): both are attempts to say "in virtue of"
without saying "causes" and without settling for "necessitates."
