Two theses that sound alike and are not.

- **Existence monism.** Exactly one concrete object exists — the world. Tables
  and people are not parts of it but ways it is (adverbial or "modal"
  readings). Parmenides is the ancestor; Horgan and Potrč the modern defence.
- **Priority monism.** Many objects exist; exactly one is *fundamental*, namely
  the whole cosmos, and its parts are [grounded](grounding.md) in it rather than
  the reverse. Schaffer's revival.

The second is the interesting one, because it accepts the ordinary inventory and
disputes only the direction of [dependence](fundamentality.md). Its arguments:
the cosmos is prior to arbitrary undetached parts; quantum entanglement makes
the state of a whole not determined by its parts; and a "gunky" world with no
atoms would leave pluralism without a foundation while monism is untroubled.

## Spinoza's version

[Spinoza](../lineage/spinoza.md) reaches monism from the definition of
substance, not from physics: a substance is *in itself* and *conceived through
itself*, two substances sharing an attribute would be indiscernible, and a
substance cannot be produced by another — so there is exactly one, *Deus sive
Natura*, and everything else is a mode of it. That is an argument entirely
inside the Aristotelian vocabulary of
[substance](../aristotle/substance.md) turned against Aristotle's pluralism of
substances.

The extreme opposite pole is Aristotle's own: many independent substances, held
together not by being parts of one thing but by all standing in relation to one
[unmoved mover](../aristotle/unmoved-mover.md) — unity "with reference to one
thing," not "in accord with one thing"
([pros hen](../aristotle/pros-hen-equivocity.md)).
