Β delineates the subject matter of [first philosophy](first-philosophy.md) in a
different way from Α, Γ or Ε: by listing the problems
([aporiai](../glossary/aporia.md)) it hopes to deal with. Characteristic of
them, Aristotle says, is that they tie our thinking up in knots. He then argues
both sides of each, and in later books takes up many again — often without a
visible verdict. It is quite possible he did not think the treatise settles them
all.

| Aporia | Where it resurfaces |
|---|---|
| Are sensible substances the only ones, or are there others? | Ζ.2, Λ.6 → [unmoved mover](unmoved-mover.md) |
| Are the elements and principles kinds or individuals — and if kinds, the most generic or most specific? | Ζ.13 → [universals and substance](universals-and-substance.md) |
| Is there a cause apart from matter? | Ζ.17 → [substantial form](substantial-form.md) |
| Is there anything apart from material compounds? | Λ → [unmoved mover](unmoved-mover.md) |
| Are the principles limited in number or kind? | Λ.8 (forty-nine movers) |
| Are the principles of perishable things themselves perishable? | Θ.8 → [priority of actuality](priority-of-actuality.md) |
| Are the principles universal or particular, potential or actual? | Β.6, M.10 → [primacy dilemma](primacy-dilemma.md) |
| Are mathematical objects substances, and separate from sensible things? | Μ–Ν → [mathematical abstractionism](mathematical-abstractionism.md) |
| "The hardest and most perplexing of all": are unity and being the substance of things, or attributes of some other subject? | Γ.2, Ι → [pros hen equivocity](pros-hen-equivocity.md) |

Reading Β as the treatise's agenda is what makes the [book
map](metaphysics-treatise.md) legible: the later books are answers looking for
their questions, and Β is where the questions are.
