Ζ.10 endorses a principle about definitions and their parts: "a definition is
an account, and every account has parts, and as the account is to the thing, so
the part of the account is to the part of the thing" (1034b20–22). The
definition of a thing includes the definitions of its parts, and since only
[universals](universals-and-substance.md) are definable, the analysis
terminates in simple undefinable terms.

## The problem this makes for matter

If matter is a part of a compound, the definition of the compound would have to
include definitions of its material components — and that would sever
[essence](essence.md) from [form](substantial-form.md). Aristotle's counter is
the circle: a circle can be divided into two semicircles, but *semicircle* is
defined via *circle*, not the reverse (1035b9). Otherwise semicircles would be
defined by quarter-circles and so on, and *circle* would never be definable at
all.

His solution is to specify carefully the whole of which the matter is a part.
"Of the compound statue the bronze is a part, but of what is said to be a
statue as form it is not a part" (1035a6). What is divided into matter "is not
the substance but the compound" (1035b20–21).

It works only partway. Statues need *some* matter but no particular kind, so
bronze drops out of the essence. But "the form of the human is always found in
flesh and bones and parts of this sort" (1036b4): some things "are this in
this" (1036b23) and cannot be defined without reference to their material
parts. Aristotle's likely escape is that a *kind* of matter is itself formally
analysable, hence a predicate rather than a subject — the bare subject,
[prime matter](prime-matter.md), never enters a definition, being indefinite.

## The rule of division

Ζ.12 asks why *two-footed animal* is one thing rather than two (1037b13–14).
The proposal: "the division should take the differentia of the differentia"
(1038a9) — having divided *animal* by *footed*, divide next by *cloven-footed*,
not by something unrelated. Then "the ultimate differentia will be the
substance of the thing and its definition" (1038a19), since each differentia
entails its predecessor and listing them all merely repeats ("footed two-footed
animal … saying the same thing several times over," 1038a22–24).

This reduces the chain to one term. It does not explain why *genus +
differentia* is one — see [the unity of definition](unity-of-definition.md),
where Η.6 tries again with different tools.
