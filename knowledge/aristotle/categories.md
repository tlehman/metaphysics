Long filed among the logical works, the *Categories* opens with something
larger than logic: a general and exhaustive account of *ta onta*, the things
there are. They divide into ten kinds. Substance ([ousia](../glossary/ousia.md))
is the first; quality, quantity and relation are among the other nine.

## Two relations, not one

The scheme runs on two structurally different relations, and conflating them
ruins it.

- **Inherence** — "being *in* a subject." A non-substance "is in something,
  not as a part, and cannot exist separately from what it is in"
  (*Cat.* 1a25). Color is in bodies, knowledge in the soul. This relation
  crosses categories.
- **"Said of" a subject.** The relation of a more general to a less general
  item *within* one category, and it is transitive: animal is said of man, man
  of this man, therefore animal of this man (*Cat.* 1b10).

Each category is thus an upside-down tree — most generic kinds at the trunk,
[particulars](../being/universals-and-particulars.md) at the leaves.

## Primary substances

Individuals in the category of substance — this man, that horse — are *primary
substances*: "what is neither in a subject nor said of a subject" (2a10).
Aristotle argues (2a35–2b7) that without them nothing else would exist. Species
and genera are *secondary* substances, being the ways primary substances are
classified; members of non-substance categories depend on primary substances by
inherence. So primary substances are the basic things there are.

The [*Metaphysics*](metaphysics-treatise.md) does not simply inherit this. It
declines to take the *Categories*' examples or its
[negative criterion](subject-criterion.md) for granted, imports matter and form
from the *Physics*, and ends up demoting the very species the *Categories*
called substances (Ζ.10, 1035b29–30). See
[substantial form](substantial-form.md).
