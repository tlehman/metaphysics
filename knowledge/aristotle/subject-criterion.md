A subject is "that of which the other things are said, but which is itself not
further said of any other thing" (1028b36). That sounds like the
[*Categories*](categories.md) criterion restated, but two things have changed.

First, the question is no longer *which* things are substances but what **the
substance of** a thing is. Second, the subject Ζ.3 has in mind is matter, form,
or the compound — concepts imported from the *Physics*, absent from the
*Categories* ontology.

## Two notions of subject

| Work | Subject of | Persists through |
|---|---|---|
| *Categories* | predication | — |
| *Physics* | change | generation and destruction |

Every subject of change is a subject of predication; not conversely (six is
even, seven is prime, but numbers do not change). The *Categories* treated
individual substances as essentially simple unanalysable atoms: *this horse is
white* has predicative structure, but *this is a horse* was a brute fact with
none. The *Physics* denies that — individuals are
[hylomorphic compounds](hylomorphism.md), and *this is a horse* has structure
after all.

## The regress

Take the criterion seriously and it runs away. Form is predicated of matter as
subject, so strip predicates from a compound in thought; what remains is a
subject that is nothing in its own right, all of whose properties are accidental
to it (1029a12–27). That is [prime matter](prime-matter.md).

Aristotle rejects the result as impossible (1029a28). Substance must be
*separable* (*chôriston* — [capable of independent
existence](../glossary/choriston.md)) and a *this something*
([tode ti](../glossary/tode-ti.md) — a determinate individual). Matter can be
each, never both. The wood of a desk existed before the desk and may survive it,
so it is separable — but then it is just a quantity of a kind of stuff, no
definite individual. Construe it as the individual desk, and it is no longer
separable from the form that makes it one.

So the criterion fails, and the enquiry moves to [essence](essence.md).
