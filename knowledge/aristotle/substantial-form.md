The convergence point. [Essence](essence.md) is what the substance of a thing
is; in a [hylomorphic](hylomorphism.md) context essence is form; therefore the
substance of a thing is its form. "By the substance without matter I mean the
essence" (1032b14). It is form that makes a thing the kind of thing it is, and
so form that meets the condition [substance](substance.md) was required to
meet.

## Form as cause of being

Ζ.17 restages this as a claim about explanation. A substance is a
"starting-point and cause" ([archê kai aitia](../glossary/arche.md), 1041a9)
of being, and the job of such a cause is to explain why one thing belongs to
another. But "why is this a man?" looks unanswerable — only a man can be a man,
so we seem to be asking why a man is a man, which every thing answers by being
itself (1041a17–20).

The hylomorphic analysis rephrases the question so it has content: "why are
these — brick and stones — a house?" (1041a26). The answer is the form
predicated of that matter. Sometimes the essence is also the
[final cause](four-causes.md) (a house, a bed), sometimes the efficient cause;
in every case "what is being looked for is the cause in virtue of which the
matter is something — and this is the substance" (1041b6–9).

So a species predication (*Callias is a man*) is explained by an underlying
*form* predication whose subject is not the compound but its matter. Form
predications are the more basic. A substantial form, as a primary definable, is
its own substance — essentially predicated of itself alone — and is the
substance of a compound only derivatively, by being predicated (accidentally) of
that compound's matter.

That last distinction is what lets Aristotle survive
[Ζ.13's attack on universals](universals-and-substance.md): a universal form
is not the substance *of its instances*, and the argument at 1038b11–15 does not
actually establish more.

Notice also the priority of form to matter: "the soul of animals … is the
substance that is in accord with the account and is the form and the essence"
(1035b14–15), and "the soul is primary substance, whereas the body is matter"
(1037a5).
