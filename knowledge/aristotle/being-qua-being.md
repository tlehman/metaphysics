The phrase invites a misreading it does not deserve. It looks like it names two
things — a study, and a recondite subject matter "being qua being." It names
three: a study, a subject matter (*beings*, the things that can be said to be),
and a manner of studying them (*qua* being).

*Qua* renders Greek *hêi*, "in so far as" or "under the aspect." A study of
*x qua y* attends solely to the *y* aspect of *x*. Other sciences also study
beings, under narrower aspects — the natural scientist studies things *qua*
movable, the mathematician studies them *qua* countable and measurable (see
[mathematical abstractionism](mathematical-abstractionism.md)). The
metaphysician studies the very same things under the widest aspect there is:
*qua* beings.

## Why this is not a science of everything and therefore nothing

A single science needs a single kind with a single definition. "Being" is
[said in many ways](pros-hen-equivocity.md) — so the science seems to founder
on equivocation before it starts. Γ.2 answers that the many ways are not
*merely* homonymous: they radiate from one central case. That central case is
[substance](substance.md), which is why the science of being qua being turns
out to be, in practice, the science of substance — and why "what is being?"
collapses into "[what is substance?](substance.md)" (Ζ.1, 1028b4).

The [glossary term](../glossary/on.md) collects the Greek. Compare the modern
descendants of the question in [ontology](../being/ontology.md) and
[existence](../being/existence.md).
