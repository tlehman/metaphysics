The name is traditional scholarship's, not Aristotle's. Ζ.3 reaches the notion
by following the [subject criterion](subject-criterion.md) to its end: peel off
every predicate of a compound and what remains is "nothing at all in its own
right," an entity all of whose properties are accidental to it (1029a12–27).

Two things are worth keeping straight.

1. **Aristotle does not here say there is such a thing.** The passage produces
   it as the unacceptable consequence of a criterion he is about to reject, and
   does not indicate whether he believes prime matter exists. Whether the corpus
   is committed to it elsewhere is a long-running dispute.
2. **It is by construction indefinable.** Ζ.11 closes with exactly this: if by
   "matter" one means the ultimate subject of Ζ.3, no definition will refer to
   it, "for it is indefinite" (1037a27). That is what keeps
   [definitions](definition-by-division.md) formal even where they must mention
   a *kind* of matter.

A kind of matter is a different animal. Bronze is analysable in turn — copper
and tin in a certain ratio ([logos](../glossary/logos.md)) — so a reference to
bronze in a definition is a reference to a predicate, not to a bare subject.
The regress bottoms out only in something indefinite, which is why it cannot be
[substance](substance.md).
