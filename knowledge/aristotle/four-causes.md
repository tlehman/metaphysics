[Aitia](../glossary/aitia.md) is better rendered "explanation" than "cause," and
like *being* it is [said in many ways](pros-hen-equivocity.md). The canonical
statement is *Physics* II.3 194b24–33, restated at *Metaphysics* Α.3 and Δ.2.

| Cause | Aristotle's phrase | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Material | "that out of which a thing comes to be, and which persists" | bronze, silver, of a statue or bowl |
| Formal | "the form … the account of the essence" | the design of the house |
| Efficient | "the primary source of change or rest" | the adviser of the action; the father of the child |
| Final | "the end ([telos](../glossary/telos.md)), that for which a thing is done" | health, of walking |

The labels are traditional; Aristotle uses none of them.

## Reciprocity and coincidence

"Things may be causes of one another — hard work of fitness, and fitness of
hard work — although not in the same sense" (195a10): hard work is the
efficient cause of fitness, fitness the final cause of hard work.

More importantly, one and the same thing can be a cause in several senses:
"form, mover, and *telos* often coincide" (198a25). In *De Anima* the soul,
which is the form of a living thing, "is a cause in three of the ways we have
distinguished" (415b10) — formal, efficient and final at once. This is why
[substantial form](substantial-form.md) can be *the* cause of a substance's
being, and why [actuality is prior](priority-of-actuality.md): an acorn's
efficient cause is the actual oak that produced it, its formal cause the
[logos](../glossary/logos.md) defining that actuality, its final cause the
mature oak it develops into.

Modern metaphysics keeps the *structure* of this multi-sense explanatory
priority while dropping the taxonomy — see [grounding](../being/grounding.md).
