Having argued that the most general science studies [being qua
being](being-qua-being.md), Γ turns to the axioms used in *all* reasoning,
which no departmental science can claim. Chief among them is the PNC: "the same
thing cannot at the same time belong and also not belong to the same thing and
in the same respect" (1005b19). Aristotle calls it the most certain of all
principles, and denies it is a hypothesis.

## Why there is no proof, and what replaces one

The PNC cannot be proved, because it is employed implicitly in every proof
whatever the subject matter — a purported proof would be circular. Aristotle
does not attempt one. Instead Γ.4 argues that the PNC is **indubitable**: not
that it is a necessary truth, but that no one who has beliefs at all can believe
it false.

The argument is dialectical and turns on signification. To express a belief —
to oneself or to anyone — is to make an assertion, and to assert is to
*signify something*. But signifying something rather than another thing already
presupposes the PNC; without it, one would have no reason to think one's words
mean anything determinate. So anyone who makes any assertion has already
committed themselves to the principle. The denier is invited to speak, and by
speaking loses.

This is a different move from the deductions elsewhere in the corpus — it
targets the denier rather than the proposition, and its modern descendants run
from transcendental arguments to the debate over
[dialetheism](../references/sep-dialetheism.md), which contests the conclusion
rather than the strategy.
