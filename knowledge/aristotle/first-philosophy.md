Aristotle never called his subject "metaphysics" — the title was coined by a
first-century C.E. editor who assembled [the treatise](metaphysics-treatise.md)
and placed it *after the Physics* in the curriculum. Aristotle himself names the
inquiry four ways, and the four names are the entry's central interpretive
problem: *first philosophy*, *wisdom* ([sophia](../glossary/sophia.md)), *the
study of [being qua being](being-qua-being.md)*, and *theology*.

"First" does not mean first in the order of study. Aristotle distinguishes what
is "better known to us" from what is "better known in itself," and holds that
inquiry starts from the former and arrives at the latter (cf. *Physics*
184a16). The principles of first philosophy are better known in themselves
however remote they look from ordinary experience — which is exactly why they
come *after* the study of nature, not before it.

## The division of the theoretical sciences

Book Ε sorts the theoretical sciences by the ontological standing of their
objects:

| Science | Objects |
|---|---|
| Natural science | material, subject to change |
| Mathematics | unchanging, but not separate from matter |
| Theology | eternal, unchanging, separate from matter |

Theology is "first" and "highest" because its objects are the most independent.
That Aristotle then *identifies* this theology with the universal science of
being qua being (Ε.1, 1026a29–32) is the hardest claim in the *Metaphysics*;
the reconciliation runs through the [unmoved mover](unmoved-mover.md) and the
[primacy dilemma](primacy-dilemma.md).
