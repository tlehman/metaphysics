By the end of Ζ.11 the picture looks settled. A [substantial
form](substantial-form.md) is an [essence](essence.md), corresponds to a
species, and is what a definition's definiens denotes; only universals are
definable, so substantial forms are universals. Aristotle confirms it: Socrates
and Callias "are distinct because of their matter … but the same in form"
(1034a6–8), and being predicated of many is what makes something a universal
(*De Int.* 17a37).

Then Ζ.13 returns to the [candidate list](substance.md) and mounts a barrage of
arguments that universals are *not* substances.

## The inconsistent triad

| | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| (i) | Substance is form | Ζ.7–11, 17 |
| (ii) | Form is universal | Ζ.8, Ζ.11 |
| (iii) | No universal is a substance | Ζ.13 |

Some readers accept that Aristotle is simply committed to all three and is
inconsistent. The two main repair strategies:

- **Particular forms.** Substantial forms are not universals; each belongs
  exclusively to its own particular, so there are as many forms of a kind as
  there are individuals. (Sellars 1957, Harter 1975, Hartman 1977, Irwin 1988,
  Witt 1989b.)
- **Weakened Ζ.13.** The chapter establishes something short of (iii), leaving
  one form per species. (Woods 1967, Owen 1978, Code 1986, Loux 1991,
  Lewis 1991.)

## Where the evidence pulls

*For particular forms:* substance is "separable and this something" (Ζ.3);
there are no universals apart from their particulars (Ζ.13); universals are not
substances (Ζ.13).

*For universal forms:* substances are the definable entities *par excellence*
(Ζ.4); definition is of the universal (Ζ.11); particulars cannot be defined
(Ζ.15).

The SEP authors take the last of these to be decisive: a form unique to Callias
would yield a definition applying uniquely to Callias — defining him, which
Aristotle says cannot be done. They then defuse the other side. (1) Being a
[tode ti](../glossary/tode-ti.md) is being fully determinate, not further
differentiable; being a [kath' hekaston](../glossary/kath-hekaston.md) is being
particular and unrepeatable. Nothing forbids a *universal tode ti*. (2) "There
is not some animal beyond the particular ones" (1038b33) may mean particular
*kinds*, making the point that a genus depends on its species. (3) The argument
at 1038b11–15 assumes the substance of *x* is peculiar to *x* and concludes a
universal is not the substance of *any of its instances* — which is not the
claim that no universal is a substance. Read that way it agrees with Ζ.6: a
universal substantial form just *is* the essence it is uniquely correlated with.

The dispute has a long modern afterlife in
[realism about universals](../being/realism-about-universals.md),
[nominalism](../being/nominalism.md) and
[trope theory](../being/trope-theory.md).
